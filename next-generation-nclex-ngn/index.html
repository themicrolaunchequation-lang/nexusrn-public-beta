What is the Next Generation NCLEX?

The Next Generation NCLEX, often called NGN, is the updated NCLEX exam format designed to better measure clinical judgment and decision-making for entry-level nurses. Instead of testing only isolated facts, NGN-style questions often ask students to interpret patient information, recognize important cues, prioritize care, choose safe actions, and evaluate outcomes.

The official exam is administered through the NCLEX program. NexusRN is an independent educational resource that helps students practice NGN-style thinking. It does not provide official NCLEX exam questions.

Why did the NCLEX change?

Nursing practice requires more than remembering information. A nurse must notice when a patient is changing, decide what matters most, take safe action, and reassess the response. NGN-style testing reflects this reality by placing more emphasis on clinical judgment.

That shift is why students need to practice with case-based questions, EHR-style cues, prioritization, rationales, and multi-step thinking.

The clinical judgment thinking process

1

Recognize cues

Notice the patient findings that matter.

2

Analyze cues

Connect symptoms, vital signs, labs, orders, and history.

3

Prioritize hypotheses

Decide which problem is most likely or most urgent.

4

Generate solutions

Choose safe nursing responses that fit the patient.

5

Take action

Implement the best immediate nursing action.

6

Evaluate outcomes

Judge whether the patient improved, worsened, or needs more care.

Common NGN item types

NGN practice may include item types that require more than choosing one answer. Students should become comfortable with formats that ask them to select, match, organize, highlight, calculate, or interpret trends.

  • Case-study questions with several pieces of patient information
  • Matrix multiple choice and matrix multiple response
  • Multiple response select-all-that-apply questions
  • Drop-down cloze and case drop-down questions
  • Bow-tie style clinical judgment questions
  • Ordered response and prioritization questions
  • Highlight questions
  • Trend and calculation questions

How NGN questions are different from old NCLEX questions

Older-style questions often feel like a direct knowledge check. NGN questions can feel more like a patient-care situation: you may need to read a chart, compare findings, decide what changed, and choose the safest next step.

Old-style focus

Recall the correct fact or rule.

NGN-style focus

Use patient data to make a safe clinical judgment.

How to prepare for the Next Generation NCLEX

  1. Study the concept first. Do not jump straight into answer choices.
  2. Read the patient story carefully. Identify what is normal, abnormal, urgent, or changing.
  3. Explain your reasoning. Say why each option is safe, unsafe, priority, or not priority.
  4. Review rationales slowly. A good rationale should teach clinical judgment, not just reveal the answer.
  5. Practice across item types. Do not only practice multiple choice.
  6. Use EHR-style scenarios. Learn to pull the signal from realistic patient data.

Mini example: NGN-style thinking

A client with pneumonia has increasing respiratory rate, oxygen saturation dropping from 94% to 88%, new confusion, and increased work of breathing.

A fact-only approach might focus on defining pneumonia. An NGN clinical judgment approach asks: Which cues show deterioration? What is the priority problem? What action is safest now? What outcome would show improvement?

Clinical judgment point

The oxygen trend, increased work of breathing, and new confusion suggest worsening oxygenation. The priority is not memorizing the disease label. The priority is recognizing deterioration and responding safely.

FAQ

Is NGN a separate exam from the NCLEX?

No. NGN refers to the updated NCLEX exam format and measurement approach. It is not a separate license exam.

What does NGN test?

NGN is designed to better measure clinical judgment and decision-making using patient-care scenarios and item types that require students to interpret and act on clinical information.

Are NexusRN questions official NCLEX questions?

No. NexusRN is an independent educational practice resource. It is not affiliated with NCSBN, Pearson VUE, or any board of nursing.
